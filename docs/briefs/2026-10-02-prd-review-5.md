# Brief: PRD review 5 — adversarial review of the PRD, with recommendations, before the owner approves it

**Agent:** commit-reviewer, **Opus** (Rule 16.5: product judgement over the whole plan of record, and
adversarial — a cheaper tier would check text, not whether the plan holds). **Asked by the owner,
2026-10-02:** "for the PRD I need another adversarial review along with recommendations before I approve it."

**Under review:** `docs/PRD.md` (release 1, status Proposed, owner approval outstanding), read against
everything it depends on or that depends on it.

## Why another round

Reviews 1-4 (`docs/PRD-REVIEW.md`, `docs/PRD-REVIEW-2.md`, `docs/PRD-REVIEW-3.md`, `docs/PRD-REVIEW-4.md`)
found 19, 17, 20 and 16 findings, and in each round most blockers were created by the amendments that closed
the previous one. Review 4 turned to the code; the PRD itself has been amended since review 3 without a
whole-document reading. The owner is about to approve it as the basis for building, so this round asks
the question approval asks: **is this the right product, stated so it can be built and checked, and is
every sentence in it true?**

## What to attack

1. **Truth.** Every requirement that says something is built, decided, enforced or tested: is it, at this
   HEAD? Cite the code, migration, test, ADR or design. A sentence that was true when written and is false
   now is a finding (Rule 21.10).
2. **Consistency.** The PRD against `docs/TIERS.md`, `docs/PRICING.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md`,
   `docs/MILESTONES.md`, `docs/THREAT-MODEL.md`, `docs/SERVICE-REGISTER.md`, the ADRs in `docs/adr/`, every
   design in `docs/design/`, and the marketing site's copy under `new-app/web/`. Two documents with two
   answers is a finding, whichever is right.
3. **Buildability.** Could someone build each requirement from what is written without asking? Name each
   requirement that cannot be tested as written, and each one that silently depends on a decision nobody
   has made.
4. **Completeness for Jamaica, release 1.** What a contractor in Jamaica needs that release 1 does not
   cover or decide, judged against the PRD's own job (§3) and success measures (§10). Look hard at: GCT and
   tax on quotes and invoices; the Data Protection Act, 2020 (Jamaica) and what it asks of us as a
   processor of client data; payments and the payment-approval flow; offline use and sync; email and
   WhatsApp delivery; accessibility; and the admin console and staff access.
5. **The product bet.** §4's hard call, the tiers (§7) and the success measures (§10): does the release
   plan give the product a fair test, and would the measures tell the owner if it failed? A finding here
   is a risk with evidence, not a preference.
6. **Risks (§11).** Missing, mis-ranked, or without an owner or a trigger.
7. **Scope.** Anything in release 1 that could move to release 2 without breaking the job, and anything in
   release 2 that release 1 cannot honestly ship without.

## How to work

- Read the PRD in full first, then the documents it cites. Read code and migrations when a requirement
  claims something is built.
- Do not edit any existing file. Write your findings to a new file, docs/PRD-REVIEW-5.md (new), appending each
  finding as you find it so an interrupted run leaves what it found on disk (Rule 1.10). Do not commit.
- Number findings **B1, B2, …**. Each has: a heading `## B<n> · <one-line claim> — severity: blocker / major
  / minor / question`; a `**Where:**` line naming every file it concerns; the claim under attack, quoted;
  why it does not hold, with evidence (file and line, or a command and its output); and a
  **Recommendation** — what to change, and when there is a real choice, the options with your recommended
  one first and why.
- No real personal data and no secrets in anything you write; synthetic examples only.
- Never `git checkout --`, `git restore`, `git stash`, or commit.

## What to report

At the top of docs/PRD-REVIEW-5.md (new), after the findings are written:

1. **A recommendation to the owner, in one line:** approve as is, approve after named changes, or do not
   approve yet — and why, in a short paragraph.
2. **The changes that recommendation depends on**, ranked, each pointing to its finding.
3. **The decisions only the owner can make**, each with options and your recommended one.
4. **What this review did not examine** (Rule 21.4).

Then reply with the same summary and the path of the file.
